## Reproduction Steps
1. Run `tests/integration/network/disconnect_host_left_test.gd` alone, three times.

**Expected Result**: `host_left` fires within `enet_timeout_max_ms + ENET_PING_INTERVAL_S + tick` (3.017 s).
**Actual Result**: it fires after 3.765 s and 4.276 s in two failing runs. The sibling server-side test (`test_killed_client_is_reported_dropped...`) detects in 1.17-1.22 s every run.

## Notes
- The server side detects in about 1.2 s, while the client side takes 3.7-4.3 s. Inference: the client's peer to the host may not get the same `set_timeout` as the server's peers, or `host_left` waits on something after the disconnect. Reading where `set_timeout` is applied on the client would settle it.
- Do not widen the bound (house rule).

## Fix
- **Root cause**: not a missing `set_timeout` (the client applies it in `_on_connected_to_server`) and not the harness. ENet checks a timeout only when a retransmit fires, and the retransmit timeout doubles from rto0 = rtt + 4 * variance. With limit 32 the rto rule needs 5 doublings, so the check lands at the first retransmit instant past `max` (rto0 * (2^k - 1)). Detection therefore scales with the survivor's RTT estimate: the server's peer had rto0 about 20 ms (1.2 s), the client's about 100 ms under frame jitter (3.7-4.3 s). BUG-0003 is the same cause; the dead-peer (server side) test also failed at 4.0 s under CPU load.
- **Change**: `data/knobs/net.tres` `enet_timeout_limit` 32 -> 8, `enet_timeout_min_ms` 1000 -> 750 (max stays 2000); SHIPPED_DIGEST re-pinned; `net_knobs.gd` comment records why. Worst case is about 2 * min + rto0 after the first unacknowledged ping.
- **Regression test**: the test now inflates the client's RTT estimate (`_jitter_rtt`: ping plus a 40 ms stalled frame, six times; rtt 23 ms, variance 20 ms) so it fails deterministically on the old knobs.
- **Timings** (host_left after kill): before 3.69-3.82 s (3 of 3 fail, bound 3.017); after 1.99-2.11 s (13 of 13 pass). Unjittered: before 2.1-2.4 s, after 1.3-1.7 s.
- **Inference**: shorter min/limit raises false-positive risk on real lossy links; NAV-14 (real-network timing) settles it.
